# ⚖️ Professional Practice: The CCTV Ethics Case

**Course:** CIT 507, Professional Practice in Information Technology · Assessment 2

## Scenario
A small-town retailer facing rising shoplifting wants to (1) install CCTV, (2) post images of suspected shoplifters on social media, and (3) ban alleged offenders. As the IT professional, should you just build what the customer asks for?

## Frameworks I applied

**ITP Code of Ethics:** good faith, integrity, community focus, and skills and professional development. Integrity means using judgement instead of just following instructions, and community focus means weighing the retailer's interests against effects on the public.

**ITIL 4:** *Service Request Management* (clarify the real need behind "install CCTV") and *Change Enablement* (assess risk, document and approve before implementing).

**New Zealand legislation:**
- **Privacy Act 2020**: CCTV footage of people is personal information, and collection needs a defined purpose. Collecting footage is different from publishing it.
- **Harmful Digital Communications Act 2015**: communication principles on false allegations, harassment and disclosing sensitive personal facts.
- **Copyright Act 1994**: access isn't permission to copy or distribute.
- **Health and Safety at Work Act 2015**: working at height, electrical and cabling hazards during installation.

**Societal expectations:** privacy, accessibility and digital inclusion, environmental impact (e-waste), and cultural sensitivity (Te Tiriti o Waitangi, Māori Data Sovereignty).

## My analysis
I separated the request into three questions instead of calling it all "acceptable" or "unacceptable":

1. **Should the retailer improve security?** Yes, the business need is legitimate.
2. **Can CCTV be implemented responsibly?** Yes, with a defined purpose and controls on access, storage, retention and use.
3. **Should the IT professional automatically enable public posting of suspects?** No. It changes the purpose and risk (mistaken identity, harassment, reputational harm that spreads fast in a small community) and needs privacy and legal review first.

## Recommended approach
A proportionate, documented setup:
- CCTV for security and incident investigation
- Restricted access to footage and defined retention periods
- Clear staff procedures and training
- Signage and notification
- Report suspected offences to Police
- Physical measures such as store layout
- Privacy Impact Assessment and legal advice **before** any public disclosure

## Takeaways
Technical capability doesn't equal professional or legal permission. A good IT professional documents concerns, explains risks to the client, and knows when to refer to specialist advice.
